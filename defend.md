# UCR Housing Conduct Meeting — Prep Checklist

## 1. Right After Contacted
- [ ] Note the date you were contacted (should be within 3 business days of the report being filed)
- [ ] Confirm which type of meeting it is, if stated: Advisory/Administrative Meeting (informal) or Administrative Review Meeting (formal — this is where responsibility is decided)
- [ ] Check the letter for the meeting date/time — you're entitled to at least 24 hours notice
- [ ] Decide if you want to bring an adviser
  - [ ] If yes: notify the Conduct Administrator **at least 24 hours before the meeting**
- [ ] Request to review the incident report and any supporting documentation before the meeting

## 2. Gather Evidence
- [ ] Timeline of what happened, written out while it's fresh
- [ ] Relevant texts, photos, videos, emails
- [ ] Names/contact info of any witnesses who can support your account
  - [ ] Ask witnesses if they're willing to attend, or willing to be contacted by staff directly
- [ ] Anything showing you weren't properly notified, if notice/timing was an issue
- [ ] Any documentation of medical, disability, or other accommodation needs relevant to the incident

## 3. Before Walking In
- [ ] Know the specific policy number(s) you're alleged to have violated
- [ ] Read the exact policy language yourself — don't rely on secondhand summary
- [ ] Identify anything ambiguous in that policy's wording that might apply to your situation
- [ ] Prepare a short, factual account of what happened — stick to specifics, not general character statements
- [ ] If defending a friend as a witness/adviser: know you can support them, but they have to respond to the allegations themselves — you can't do it for them

## 4. During the Meeting
- [ ] Present your account clearly and factually
- [ ] Bring up witnesses or hand over their contact info if not already done
- [ ] Present your documents/evidence
- [ ] Ask questions if anything about the process or allegations is unclear
- [ ] Take notes on what's said, especially anything about next steps or timelines

## 5. After the Meeting
- [ ] You'll get an email/decision letter summarizing the meeting and outcome
- [ ] If found responsible: review the sanctions and note any deadlines to complete them
- [ ] Consider filing a **written reply** to the decision — this goes in your file regardless of outcome, even if you're not appealing
- [ ] If you disagree with the outcome, check the clock: **appeal must be filed within 3 business days of the decision letter**

## 6. If Appealing
Only three grounds qualify — make sure your appeal clearly fits one:
- [ ] **Severity**: The sanction is disproportionate to what you were found responsible for
- [ ] **New evidence**: Something wasn't available at the time of the hearing
- [ ] **Due process**: Your rights in the process weren't honored (e.g., no proper notice, no chance to respond, no access to the report)

- [ ] Use the appeal link provided in your decision letter
- [ ] Write the appeal around your strongest documented ground — don't pad it with general fairness arguments, they carry less weight
- [ ] Keep a copy of everything you submit

## Important
- Not attending a meeting does **not** protect you, a decision can be made without your input, and an academic hold can follow
- Appeals are a review of process, not a full rehearing, unless the ground is new evidence
- Parental notification only happens automatically for underage drinking or drug possession findings
- Check UCR student email regularly, it's the official channel for all of this
